SBTi Corporate Net-Zero Standard V2

What changed in the V2 second draft, what it means scope by scope, and the decisions companies need to make now.

AuthorAngus Harman IssuedJune 2026 StandardFinal V2.0 — June 2026
Written by Angus Harman

The SBTi has published the final Corporate Net-Zero Standard V2.0 (CNZS V2.0), operationalising its 2026–2030 strategy. Since its establishment in 2015, SBTi has grown to over 10,000 companies with validated targets (and 13,000+ including commitments), cementing its position as the global benchmark for corporate climate target-setting.

As scale has increased, the SBTi has evolved its approach, moving from a generalised framework to more sector- and geography-specific guidance, with stronger alignment across adjacent frameworks and clearer implementation pathways. Notably, it positions itself — in its own words — as a "transformation partner" rather than an ambition-setter, placing greater emphasis on delivery, accountability and real-economy impact.

This article outlines the key changes and what companies need to act on now.

Executive Summary

The Key SBTi Corporate Net Zero Updates

01 · Operational

A formal Category A / B split

Category A: Scope 1–3 targets, third-party assurance, published transition plan, post-2035 removals. Category B is materially lighter on all counts. The old SME route is gone.

02 · Governance

Set-and-forget is gone

All companies publish transition plans with board sign-off. Annual progress reporting is mandatory. Baselines reset every five years with public performance disclosure.

03 · Scope 1

Scope 1 gets its own target

Three target methods: Absolute Reduction, Sector-Specific, and the new Asset Decarbonisation Plan. Separating Scope 1 from Scope 2 removes the ability to cover a Scope 1 shortfall with cheap REC purchases and report a clean blended figure.

04 · Scope 2

Renewable claims require a credible energy strategy

Generator age limits, deliverability region matching, and hourly reporting requirements disqualify much of what companies have been counting.

05 · Scope 3

Focus on priority emissions, not all of them

Targets cover only categories representing ≥5% of total Scope 3. Supplier alignment targets let counterparties formally assume responsibility for shared emissions via written agreement, making who you buy from a climate strategy decision.

06 · Carbon

OER replaces BVCM with a three-tier framework

BVCM is replaced by the Ongoing Emissions Responsibility (OER) framework, which introduces three recognition tiers: Engaged, Advanced and Leadership. The programme is optional to 2035, after which Category A companies are required to take responsibility for a defined share of ongoing emissions, including support for eligible removals. Companies must declare their participation (or non-participation) at validation, with disclosure captured on the SBTi Dashboard.

What to do now

5 key actions for companies to make now

V2.0 is operative from February 2027, but most of these decisions have lead times longer than that. Acting after the standard takes effect means acting too late.

01

Model your removals obligation now

From 2035, Category A companies must cover ongoing Scope 1–3 emissions with removals: 1% rising to 100% by the net-zero year, with the durable share compounding from 10% to 100% over the same period. That's a year-by-year volume schedule. It belongs in procurement planning now, not when the 2035 trigger arrives.

02

Set your OER position before your next validation

Participation is voluntary, but abstention is a public filing. Three levels: Engaged (≥1%), Advanced (100% Scope 1–2 plus ≥10% total), Leadership (100% at $80/t with full credit retirement). The right answer depends on ambition, supply access, and cost modelling. The decision needs to be made before the Dashboard declaration, not after.

03

Lock in durable removal supply before 2030

Over 90% of available industrial biochar supply is already contracted. Long-lived removal capacity is the scarcest part of the market, and the durability ramp concentrates demand from 2035. Contract structure matters: delivery-matched payment preserves capital until the obligation lands. Waiting until 2033 to procure means bidding into a seller's market with no alternatives.

04

Map which suppliers are SBTi-aligned and formalise it

A supplier target changes who is responsible for shared emissions, not whether they appear in your inventory. Volume from an aligned supplier counts toward your Scope 3 target. A supplier with its own validated target can assume responsibility via written agreement. That makes counterparty alignment a climate accounting decision. The categories worth mapping first are those with stable, long-term relationships. Not all Scope 3 categories will support the written agreement structure V2.0 requires.

05

Audit your Scope 2 certificate holdings against the new rules

Generator age, regional matching, and hourly reporting requirements will disqualify much of what's currently on the books. In Australia, most LGC-backed claims won't pass. SBTi compliance is voluntary; ASRS disclosure is not. These are separate questions. The relevant one for clients disclosing under ASRS is whether the accounting basis of their renewable electricity position remains accurate, not whether it satisfies SBTi's matching rules. The audit needs to happen before the next disclosure cycle, not after it.

Deep Dive

V2.0 Roadmap

The window is shorter than it looks

The pressure point is 2035, not 2050. That's when Category A removals become mandatory, and when durable supply, already 90%+ contracted by a handful of large buyers, gets genuinely scarce. Companies treating 2035 as the planning horizon are already late to the procurement decisions that matter.

All new submissions and revalidations must comply with V2.0. V1.3.1 submissions must be lodged before this date.

Companies start setting next-cycle near-term targets for 2030–2035. End-of-cycle public reporting commences for current-cycle targets.

Near-Term Target Deadline

Performance against near-term targets becomes public for many companies that set targets under the old guidance.

2035

OER Mandatory for Category A

Ongoing Emissions Responsibility kicks in at 1% of Scope 1–3 emissions, rising to 100% at the net-zero year. The durable removal share runs from 10% to 100% over the same period. Durable supply is already constrained.

2050

100% Removal Coverage Required

All residual emissions must be neutralised by removals. 100% durable storage required. Companies that didn't lock in long-lived supply early are bidding into a market with almost no headroom.

Section 01 · Operational Changes

Company requirements

Annual progress reporting, 5-year target reviews, third-party assurance, and published transition plans are now standing requirements for Category A. Validation is the start, not the finish.

Three other changes worth flagging

Governance

Transition plans: public, board-signed, assured

Category A: public disclosure with board sign-off and third-party assurance within 15 months of validation. Category B: board-approve only, disclosure recommended. This isn't a template exercise. The plan has to show how the targets get delivered.

Reporting

Baselines move. Targets get reviewed every five years.

Baselines are no longer frozen at validation. Annual progress data is publicly disclosed. At the end of each 5-year cycle, performance against targets becomes part of the public record. Minimum progress criteria will follow; they won't apply until 2035, but they're coming.

New · Framework

OER replaces BVCM

Beyond Value Chain Mitigation and neutralisation are folded into one framework. Three voluntary tiers pre-2035, a mandatory floor from 2035 for Category A, and full neutralisation required at net-zero year.

Section 02 · Scope 1

Scope 1 gets its own target

Fuel combustion, fleets, process emissions, fugitive gases: each separated out, with three methods to set the target. The blended Scope 1+2 number is gone. You can no longer cover a Scope 1 gap with cheap REC purchases and report a clean combined figure.

Method 01

Absolute Reduction

Straight-line percentage reduction in absolute tonnes against a base year, in line with a 1.5°C trajectory. The default route for most sectors.

Method 02

Sector-Specific

Targets set against a sector-specific intensity benchmark. Cement, steel, aluminium, power, aviation, and shipping where well-defined sector pathways exist. Transition plan required at validation.

Method 03 · New

Asset Decarbonisation Plan

An asset-level schedule to abate, retire, or replace GHG-emitting assets in line with net zero by 2050. Must be paired with a carbon budget that caps total tonnes over the period. This is the method designed for hard-to-abate operations where legacy assets run on long timelines.

The Lens

The separation is a credibility call. Folding Scope 1 into a combined target let companies paper over hard-to-abate process emissions with cheap Scope 2 abatement. A company running a heavy industrial asset could buy LGCs, hit its blended target, and call it done. That's closed.

The ADP makes sense for anyone with long-lived, hard-to-abate assets: it lets you phase retirements across a realistic timeline instead of forcing a linear straight-line on assets that can't decarbonise that way. But the carbon budget pairing is non-negotiable: without it, the ADP is a flexibility mechanism without a ceiling. The budget is where the accountability sits.

Section 03 · Scope 2

Renewable claims become harder without a credible energy strategy

Age limits, regional matching, and hourly reporting requirements will disqualify much of what companies have been counting. Several changes, each targeting a different way that companies have been using certificate markets to claim clean electricity without much additionality to show for it.

Rule 01

Rising low-carbon electricity targets

LCE targets rise on a linear trajectory aligned to an eligible net-zero pathway. 100% low-carbon electricity required by 2040. Companies with >20% electricity growth over the target cycle must set a Scope 2 emissions target.

Rule 02

Generator age limit: 15 years

The generating asset must have been built or re-powered within 15 years of the reporting period. Old generation doesn't count. This strips out the cheap, unbundled certificate stock that underpinned most "100% renewable" claims.

Rule 03

Same deliverability region

Certificates must come from generators in the same deliverability region as consumption. A company in one grid zone can't buy certificates from a generator in an unconnected zone and claim renewable electricity.

New · Integrity

Hourly matching: mandatory reporting for large loads

Loads of ≥10 GWh must report their hourly-matched share. SBTi recognition tiers: ≥50% hourly by 2030, ≥75% by 2035, ≥90% from 2035. One matching interval per pool; no mixing hourly and annual within the same pool to cherry-pick clean hours.

New · Definition

Low-carbon is an emissions threshold, not a technology label

0.048 kg CO₂/kWh now. Tightens to 0.024 kg CO₂/kWh in 2035. Whether a generator qualifies is a function of what it actually emits, not how it's classified.

Rule 04

Consumption projections mandatory for Category A

Category A companies must estimate and submit projected electricity consumption over the target timeframe. Data demand goes up significantly.

The Lens

In Australia, a "100% renewable" claim typically rests on LGCs, often cheap unbundled certificates from old projects matched once a year. That's precisely what V2.0 disqualifies: too old, wrong matching interval, possible geographic mismatch. The certificate volume that underpins most corporate renewable claims in this market is at risk.

SBTi is voluntary. ASRS and AASB S2 are not, but they are separate obligations. A certificate stock that fails V2.0's Scope 2 matching rules doesn't automatically create an ASRS exposure. The ASRS question is whether disclosed climate positions are accurate on the basis on which they were made. For clients with renewable electricity claims built on LGCs that V2.0 would disqualify, the practical question is narrower: does the existing portfolio support the SBTi target being claimed, and if it doesn't, what does that mean for the transition plan they're required to disclose?

Section 04 · Scope 3

Focus on priority emissions, not all of them

The ≥5% threshold focuses targets on categories where companies have real influence. The counterparty alignment option adds a new mechanic: a supplier with its own validated SBTi target can formally assume responsibility for shared emissions via written agreement. That shifts supplier selection from a procurement question to a climate strategy one.

Key Change · Coverage

5% priority threshold

Near-term targets must cover every Scope 3 category representing ≥5% of total. Mandatory for Category A. Justified exclusions permitted for non-material or low-influence categories; eight specific exclusions are listed in the standard.

Target Type 01

Absolute Emissions

Linear trajectory to a defined residual level consistent with an eligible net-zero pathway.

New · Target Type 02

Supplier / customer alignment

Share of tier-1 suppliers and/or customers (by emissions, spend, or revenue) that are in transition or net-zero aligned. Where a written agreement allocates coverage, an aligned partner can assume responsibility for those shared emissions.

New · Target Type 03

Category or activity-specific

Targets applied to specific Scope 3 categories or activities (steel, cement, chemicals), individually or in combination. Particularly relevant for companies identifying Emissions Intensive Activities across their value chains.

The Lens

The ≥5% threshold is the right call. Too many companies have been burning resource calculating emissions across low-materiality categories they have no real leverage over, partly because the old rules left no clean basis for exclusion. Focusing on priority sources should shift effort toward categories where action is actually possible.

The supplier alignment mechanic is the bigger change. A supplier target doesn't remove emissions from your inventory; it changes who is on the hook for them. Where a written agreement allocates coverage, the aligned counterparty formally assumes responsibility. That makes supplier relationships a climate accounting question, not just a procurement one. Companies need to map which of their Scope 3 categories have supplier relationships stable enough to underpin a written agreement. Not all of them will.

Section 05 · Ongoing Emissions Responsibility

Three optional recognition tiers. OER mandatory for Category A from 2035.

BVCM is replaced by a three-tier recognition framework: Engaged, Advanced, and Leadership. All three tiers are voluntary. From 2035, Category A companies must participate at a minimum — covering at least 1% of ongoing Scope 1–3 emissions. Companies declare their position at validation; declining requires a written rationale filed to the SBTi Dashboard.

Engaged
~$20/tCO₂e

≥1% of ongoing Scope 1, 2 and 3 emissions addressed via mitigation contributions. Recommended price; no mandated floor. Optional pre-2035; mandatory for Category A from 2035 at a minimum.

Advanced
$20+/tCO₂e

100% of Scope 1 and 2, plus additional Scope 3 such that total coverage reaches at least 10% of all ongoing emissions.

Leadership
$80+/tCO₂e

100% of total ongoing Scope 1, 2 and 3 emissions. Progressive disbursement recommended: commit funds progressively over the target period, not deferred to year-end. Verified mitigation outcomes required in volume equal to covered emissions.

* Category B: Leadership threshold is 10% of total ongoing emissions, not 100%. No 2035 mandatory floor.

The post-2035 removal ramp for Category A

From 2035: coverage rises from 1% to 100% of ongoing Scope 1–3. Simultaneously, the durable share within that coverage rises from 10% to 100%. Both ramps run to the net-zero year; the obligation compounds.

The Lens

Durable CDR retirements were under 200,000 tonnes in 2025, 0.11% of total retirements. Supply is already effectively allocated: over 90% of available industrial biochar supply was contracted by 2025, concentrated in a small number of large buyers including Microsoft and Google.

Category A companies with 2035 exposure that wait until 2033 to procure will be bidding against everyone else into a market with almost no discretionary headroom. Leadership is not a reputational play. It's a financial commitment. $80/t is the floor, 100% coverage is the requirement, and the durable share rises every year. Model the all-in multi-year cost before declaring a tier.

SBTi-aligned companies cover an estimated 34 billion tCO₂e across Scopes 1–3. In 2025, 20 million tonnes of carbon credits were retired against that, 0.06% of covered emissions. V2.0 forces that number to move.

Section 06 · Neutralisation

At net-zero year, the type of removal is the constraint

CO₂ and N₂O, both long-lived GHGs, must be cancelled with durable, long-lived removals. Nature-based doesn't qualify. The qualifying set is DAC and geological storage: $300–1,000+/t and supply-constrained. A company with a 2040 net-zero year has five years from the 2035 mandatory ramp to reach full durable coverage. The procurement decision belongs in 2026, not 2034.

Rule · Scope 1 Residuals

Direct responsibility

Companies must directly neutralise all Scope 1 residual emissions. Durable, long-lived removals only, defined as carbon retention over centuries to millennia.

Rule · Scope 3 Residuals

Joint or direct neutralisation

All Scope 3 residuals must be neutralised: jointly with value-chain partners that report the same emissions, or directly where a partner hasn't demonstrated coverage. Written agreements are required where coverage is shared.

Rule · Permanence Matching

Long-lived emissions need long-lived storage

CO₂ and N₂O must be cancelled with durable, long-lived removals. Methane, being short-lived, can use either. Scope 2 emissions are projected to be negligible beyond 2040.

Integrity · NDC

Avoid NDC double-counting

Recommended to use removals not simultaneously claimed against countries' NDCs where possible. Apply corresponding adjustments where available.

The Lens

Nature-based removals at $10–50/t are not what neutralisation requires for long-lived GHG residuals. The qualifying set (DAC, enhanced weathering with long-term storage, geological storage) sits at $300–1,000+/t and is capital-intensive to bring to market. Supply at scale doesn't exist yet.

A company with a 2040 net-zero year is five years from needing 100% durable removal coverage of its residuals, on a ramp that starts at 10%. The forward liability should be modelled now: not as a 2035 procurement decision, but as a 2026 strategic one. Waiting until the ramp is active means buying into a market with no slack.
